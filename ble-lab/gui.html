Captures the advertisements the BE200 hears over the air. Following a third-party connection between two other devices across the frequency hopping and decrypting it needs dedicated sniffer hardware (Ubertooth / nRF52 / Ellisys) — no commercial combo chip, BE200 included, exposes that mode.

Press Start to discover Classic Bluetooth (BR/EDR) devices via Inquiry — address, RSSI, device class, and name (via Extended Inquiry Response), continuously re-armed until you stop. This is a separate radio job from BLE Sniff (one Bluetooth job runs at a time) — stop one before starting the other. Press Stop & save for a .pcap — a lossless container conversion of the full HCI capture (every byte preserved, nothing reconstructed), so Wireshark decodes the Inquiry Results natively with full field breakdown.

Inquiry-based discovery — the same mechanism bluetoothctl scan uses: it sees devices that respond to being paged (put the target into pairing/discoverable mode). It is not promiscuous interception of an existing Classic connection between two other devices — that needs dedicated BR/EDR protocol-analyzer hardware, well beyond any commercial combo chip including the BE200.

On a host reachable over the Wi-Fi under test, run iperf3 -s. Best result: victim = a MediaTek card, interferer = BE200 BT. Both must be on a 2.4 GHz channel.
